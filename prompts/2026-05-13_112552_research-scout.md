### 1. Concise Summary
DeepAFM extends AlphaFold‑style deep‑learning to predict **dynamic conformational changes** of proteins from atomic‑force microscopy (AFM) data. By training on large‑scale molecular dynamics simulations and experimental AFM time‑series, the model outputs probable motion pathways rather than static structures. The workflow runs on GPU‑accelerated HPC clusters, achieving ~10× speed‑up over traditional MD‑based analysis while preserving sub‑nanometer accuracy.

### 2. Operational Impact for HPC Administrators
| Impact Area | Details |
|------------|---------|
| **GPU Utilization** | DeepAFM is GPU‑heavy (CNN + transformer layers). Requires nodes with ≥4 × A100‑equivalent GPUs to hit reported throughput. |
| **Job Scheduling** | Short‑to‑medium‑length jobs (≈30 min–2 h) with high memory (≈256 GB) per node; benefits from **pre‑emptible** or **burst‑buffer** queues to absorb spikes in demand. |
| **Software Stack** | Depends on PyTorch 2.2, CUDA 12, and custom AFM‑pre‑processing libraries. Needs container images (Singularity/Apptainer) to keep dependencies isolated. |
| **Data I/O** | AFM time‑series (hundreds of MB per experiment) streamed from high‑speed parallel file system; recommends **NVMe‑based burst buffers** to avoid bottlenecks. |
| **Scalability** | Scales linearly up to 8 GPUs per node; beyond that, communication overhead dominates. Cluster‑wide scaling limited by inter‑node GPU‑direct RDMA. |
| **User Workflow** | Replaces multi‑day MD simulations with a single GPU job, reducing queue wait times and storage footprint (no need for terabytes of trajectory files). |

- **Resource planning:** Anticipate increased GPU demand from bio‑AI workloads.

- **Storage:** Reduced long‑term storage but higher burst‑buffer usage.
